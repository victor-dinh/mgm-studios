QR menu vs QR ordering: what is the difference?

Both systems start with a guest scanning a code at the table, but they solve different problems. The key is what guests and staff can do after the menu opens.

MGM Studio › Blog › QR menu vs QR ordering: what is the difference?

A QR menu replaces a paper menu

Guests browse items and prices on their phones, then tell a server what they want. It is easy to introduce because the service workflow changes very little, but staff still take orders.

  • Displays items, descriptions and prices
  • Can be updated online
  • May not have a cart or order-management screen

QR ordering submits an order

Guests choose quantities and send an order. Staff then acknowledge and manage the order in a separate interface. This can reduce manual transcription, but does not eliminate table service.

  • Cart and submit action
  • Optional table identification
  • Staff-facing order status

Choose by the problem you need to solve

If your goal is simply a digital menu, a view-only QR menu might be enough. If you want guests to submit items directly, choose an ordering solution and test the complete workflow.

  • Browse-only requirement: QR menu
  • Customer self-ordering: QR ordering
  • Inventory, receipts and payments: verify separate POS integrations

Names can be misleading

Some suppliers call full ordering products “QR menus,” while others use “ordering” for a limited workflow. Ask for a complete real-world demonstration instead of relying on the label.
